# YouTube Manager Playbook

Brand-neutral craft for running a YouTube channel: what to optimize when
publishing a video, and why. Applies to both long-form and Shorts unless a
section says otherwise.

## Titles

- **Formula: curiosity + keyword.** Lead with the specific, searchable term
  (what someone would type into the search bar), then add a curiosity gap or
  concrete stake. "FTMO Trailing Drawdown Explained (The Math Most Traders
  Get Wrong)" beats either "FTMO Trailing Drawdown Explained" or "The Math
  Most Traders Get Wrong" alone.
- **Length:** 40–60 characters is the safe zone across devices; YouTube
  truncates around 60–70 on desktop search and earlier on mobile. Front-load
  the keyword and the hook inside the first 40 characters so truncation
  never eats the payoff.
- **One idea per title.** Don't stack two hooks — pick the stronger one.
- **Avoid:** ALL CAPS spam, more than one emoji, clickbait that the video
  doesn't pay off in the first 15 seconds (this tanks retention, which then
  tanks the title's own future reach).
- **Numbers and brackets work** ("(2026 Update)", "Part 2", "in 90 Seconds")
  because they set a concrete expectation.

## Thumbnails

- **High contrast, always.** The thumbnail needs to read at 120×90px in a
  crowded feed — low-contrast or busy backgrounds disappear next to
  competitors.
- **Faces perform.** A clear human face with legible expression (surprise,
  focus, concern) consistently outperforms object-only or text-only
  thumbnails, when a face is authentic to the content.
- **Text: ≤ 3–4 words, large, high-contrast against the background.** The
  thumbnail text and the title should not repeat each other verbatim — text
  on the thumbnail should add a second angle (a number, a result, a "vs")
  that the title doesn't already say.
- **One focal point.** A thumbnail trying to show three things loses to one
  that shows one thing clearly.
- **A/B test them.** Use YouTube's built-in thumbnail testing (or manual
  swap-and-compare over a fixed window) on anything that matters — CTR
  intuition is frequently wrong, and a thumbnail swap can double CTR on an
  otherwise-identical video.
- **Brand consistency helps recognition** (a consistent color treatment or
  corner logo) but never at the cost of contrast or clarity on a given
  thumbnail.

## Descriptions

- **First 2 lines are the ad.** Only the first ~100–120 characters show
  before "Show more" in search and suggested — put the actual value
  proposition and the primary keyword there, not a channel intro.
- **Timestamps (chapters)** for anything over ~3 minutes with distinct
  sections. Chapters improve session retention (viewers jump to the part
  they want instead of leaving) and populate YouTube's own chapter UI.
- **Links:** put the single most important link (the product page, the
  next video, the playlist) right after the first two lines, before the
  description gets long. Don't bury it under a wall of boilerplate.
- **Repeat the primary keyword naturally** once or twice in the body —
  search relevance still reads description text, even though title and
  tags carry more weight.
- **Avoid:** a description that's 100% boilerplate (social links, channel
  tagline) with zero video-specific content — this wastes the highest-value
  real estate on the page.

## Tags and keywords

- Tags are a minor signal now relative to title/description/thumbnail
  click-through and watch time, but still worth doing right: use 5–8
  specific tags (the actual search terms a viewer would use), not 20 vague
  ones.
- Include one or two "broad category" tags (the niche) and several
  "specific" tags (the exact topic), rather than only one or the other.
- Never tag with a competitor's brand name to bait their audience — this
  reads as spam to viewers who do see tags and can trigger platform
  penalties.

## Shorts vs. long-form strategy

- **Shorts** are a discovery funnel, not a monetization end in themselves in
  most niches: their job is to put the channel in front of new viewers who
  then convert to subscribers or long-form watchers. Optimize Shorts for
  completion rate (watch to the end, ideally loop) over any other metric —
  the algorithm rewards near-100% completion heavily.
- **Long-form** is where depth, authority, and ad-revenue efficiency live.
  A viewer who found the channel via a Short should have an obvious
  long-form video to click into (pinned comment, end screen, or a Short
  that's explicitly a teaser/clip of the long-form piece).
- **Repurpose, don't duplicate blind.** Cutting a long-form video's single
  best 30–45 second moment into a Short works; re-uploading the same framing
  and pacing at Shorts length without re-editing the hook usually
  underperforms because Shorts viewers decide to keep watching in the first
  1–2 seconds, not 15.
- **Cadence:** Shorts can sustain near-daily publishing without audience
  fatigue since each is low-commitment; long-form works best at a
  consistent, sustainable cadence (once or twice a week) rather than
  sporadic bursts — consistency trains the algorithm's expectation of when
  to notify subscribers.

## Retention and CTR levers

- **CTR (click-through rate)** is driven almost entirely by
  thumbnail + title working together as a pair — evaluate them together, not
  separately, since a great thumbnail can be sunk by a mismatched title and
  vice versa.
- **Average view duration / retention curve** is the strongest ranking
  signal after upload. A steep drop in the first 15–30 seconds means the
  opening doesn't pay off the title/thumbnail promise — fix the cold open
  before touching anything else.
- **Pattern interrupts** (a visual change, a question to camera, a jump cut)
  every 20–40 seconds in long-form measurably reduce mid-video drop-off.
- **End screens and cards** pointing to one specific next video (not the
  whole channel) convert better than a generic "subscribe" card — give the
  viewer a single next decision.
- **Session time matters as much as single-video watch time.** A video that
  hands the viewer cleanly into another video on the channel (via end screen
  or a strong pinned-comment link) is worth more to the algorithm than one
  that lets the viewer bounce, even if its own watch time is identical.

## Publishing cadence

- Pick a cadence you can sustain for months, not a burst you can't repeat —
  irregular publishing hurts subscriber notification habits more than a
  slower-but-steady schedule.
- Publish at a consistent time of day/week once a baseline audience
  timezone is known from Analytics — small consistency gains compound.
- Batch-produce to protect the cadence during real-world disruptions; a
  content buffer of 2–4 videos protects the schedule from one bad week.

## Playlists

- Group videos by viewer intent ("start here," a topic series, a persona-fit
  set), not just chronologically — a well-named playlist is itself
  discoverable in search and suggested.
- Order playlist videos for a coherent watch session (foundational →
  advanced), since playlist autoplay is a meaningful source of session time.
- Put the channel's single best "start here" video first in the channel's
  primary playlist and link to it from the channel banner/about section.
